Queensland · 14 September – 29 September 2026

Historical crash analysis

457 crashes were recorded across Queensland in 15 days of continuous collection. They follow a clear daily rhythm, peaking in the mid-afternoon. They concentrate on the state's busiest roads, led by the Pacific Motorway. And they cluster together: a crash is followed by another within 2 km and 2 hours about twice as often as chance alone would produce.

01

Finding 1 · When

Crashes peak in the mid-afternoon.

Crashes rise with the morning commute, reaching 30 an hour at 7am, and peak at 3pm and 4pm with 33 and 34 crashes. The quietest hour is 2am, with 3.

02

Finding 2 · Where

Crashes concentrate on the busiest roads.

The Pacific Motorway (48), the Bruce Highway (42) and the Gateway Motorway (22) account for a quarter of all crashes. 329 of the 457 (72%) happened in South East Queensland.

03

Finding 3 · Together

Crashes cluster in time and place.

24 pairs of crashes happened within 2 km and 2 hours of each other: 22 of 453 crashes, about 1 in 20, were followed by another nearby. Across 5,000 simulations that kept every crash location and the real daily rhythm, chance produced 11.6 pairs on average, and only 5 simulations reached 24.

04

Limitations

What more data would add.

Traffic volumes would turn these counts into crash rates, showing which roads and hours carry the most risk per vehicle. A longer collection period would sharpen the crash-pair estimate, currently between 1.3 and 3.1 times chance, and help explain what links the pairs.

05

Method

How the data was collected and analysed.

A Python collector polls the QLDTraffic API every 60 seconds and stores every incident, and each change to it, in PostgreSQL on a cloud server. For this report, a snapshot of the database was exported and analysed in Python with pandas.
